Title: Evaluating Retrieval-Augmented Generation Integrity under Adversarial Prompt Perturbations

Abstract:
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems enhance Large Language Models (LLMs) with non-parametric memory, yet their robustness against adversarial context injection remains understudied. We evaluate three state-of-the-art RAG architectures (Dense Retrieval, Sparse BM25, and Hybrid RAG) across 10,000 queries subjected to semantic perturbations. Our findings demonstrate that hybrid retrieval mitigates context hallucination by 34.2% (p = 0.002, 95% CI [28.1%, 40.3%]) compared to pure dense methods. Complete source code, evaluation harnesses, and seeded datasets are provided for exact reproduction.

1. Research Hypotheses & Methodology
H1: Combining sparse lexical search with dense vector embeddings reduces sensitivity to out-of-domain context poisoning.
H2: Adversarial context injection degrades context precision monotonically with document insertion depth.

We collected a benchmark dataset of 10,000 multi-turn question-answering pairs across medical, legal, and financial domains. Data splits were strictly isolated into 70% train (7,000), 15% validation (1,500), and 15% test (1,500) using stratified random sampling without overlap.

2. Empirical Audit & Results
Statistical evaluations were conducted using two-tailed paired t-tests with Bonferroni correction for multiple hypothesis testing across domains.
- Sample Size: N = 1,500 test samples per domain trial, providing 99% statistical power to detect an effect size of d = 0.15 at alpha = 0.01.
- Metric Audit: Precision@k (k=5), Recall@k, Context Faithfulness Score, and Hallucination Index.
- Hybrid RAG achieved a Context Faithfulness Score of 0.887 (SD = 0.042) vs Dense RAG 0.661 (SD = 0.089), with p < 0.001.

3. Limitations & Reproducibility Statement
Key limitations include evaluating only English-language corpora and synthetic adversarial perturbations. Code, containerized Docker environments, raw model logits, and random seed initializations (seed=42) are hosted on an open-access repository.
